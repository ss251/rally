## Tool protocol

### GoalVault

[Feynman: GoalVault] This contract is a crowdfunding box. People send USDC toward a dollar target before a clock runs out. If the box fills, the named recipient takes the pile. If it misses, each backer can take their own pile back. Same-chain givers put money in themselves. Cross-chain givers’ money appears as a mint at the box, and a designated clerk writes down who it belongs to. The clerk cannot invent money that is not sitting in the box, but the clerk picks the name, the campaign, and the “which chain it came from” tag. An owner can replace the clerk, replace the cross-chain shipping desk, change shipping fees, and sweep leftover unlabeled USDC after every campaign’s extra hour has passed.

[Feynman: constructor] Stores the coin, this chain’s domain number, the first clerk, and the owner. Rejects a zero coin or a zero clerk.

[Feynman: setRelayer] Owner swaps the clerk. Next attribution and clerk-triggered refunds use the new address. In-flight unlabeled mints now wait on a different person.

[Feynman: setTokenMessenger] Owner swaps the shipping desk used to send a failed-campaign refund home. Already-credited remote backers will hit whatever desk is current when someone triggers the cross-chain refund.

[Feynman: setCctpRefundParams] Owner writes the max shipping fee and the speed/finality floor used on that desk. Already-credited remote refunds inherit the new fee/speed.

[Feynman: rescueExcess] After the latest campaign deadline plus one hour, owner sends `balance − labeled-escrow` to an address they choose. Labeled escrow cannot move this way. Unlabeled coins can.

[Feynman: createCampaign] Anyone opens a drive: target, deadline, recipient. Creator is the opener. The rescue lock is pushed out to this deadline plus one hour if that is later than the current lock. There is no maximum deadline even though a `DeadlineTooFar` error exists.

[Feynman: contribute] A same-chain giver pays now; the box measures what actually arrived and writes it under the giver on this chain. Refused after the deadline or after the recipient has already taken the pile.

[Feynman: recordContribution] Only the clerk. Writes down that some already-arrived coins belong to (campaign, person, source chain). Allowed until deadline plus one hour, unless the recipient already took the pile. Checks only that the box holds enough unlabeled coins to cover the amount.

[Feynman: withdraw] Recipient or creator pulls the campaign’s running total once that total meets the target. Does not wait for the deadline. Marks the drive closed. Pays the full running total, not “target only.”

[Feynman: refund] After a miss, a backer pulls their whole credited pile on this chain. Zeros their credit. Does not shrink the campaign’s running total.

[Feynman: refundFor] Clerk does the same local payout to a named backer.

[Feynman: refundCrossChain] Backer or clerk. On a miss, zeros the credit then, per source-chain tag, either pays locally or burns via the current shipping desk to `bytes32(that backer’s 20-byte address)` on the tagged domain. A stranger cannot call it. The clerk can.

[Feynman: _credit] Adds the amount to the campaign total, global labeled escrow, that person’s total, and that person’s per-chain bucket. Emits “goal reached” if this write is the one that crosses the target.

[Feynman: _refund] Requires a miss. Zeros the person’s credit and per-chain buckets, shrinks labeled escrow, pays the full credit to `to` on this chain.

[Feynman: _requireOpen] Drive still accepts same-chain gifts: not withdrawn, clock still before deadline.

[Feynman: _requireCreditable] Clerk may still label: not withdrawn, clock still before deadline plus one hour.

[Feynman: _requireFailed] Miss: not withdrawn, clock at/after deadline, running total still under target.

[Feynman: status / isSuccessful / isRefundable / refundableAmount] Success is “running total ≥ target” with no clock. Refundable is “clock done AND running total < target AND not withdrawn.” These two views can flip after the public deadline if the clerk still labels.

[Socratic: GoalVault.sol:353 — why?] Why may the clerk label after the public deadline? First answer: late mints. Why is there no check the mint was burned before the deadline or bound to this campaign? Implicit belief: the clerk is honest about *which* arrived coins belong *here*, and a late label is always a completion of a pre-deadline intent rather than a new economic decision.

[Socratic: GoalVault.sol:496 — why?] Why does a refund zero the person but leave `campaign.raised` untouched? First answer: raised is “how much was credited.” Why do success/fail/withdraw still read that stale total after money left? Implicit belief: once the clock says miss, nobody will increase `raised` again — but the clerk still can for an hour.

[Socratic: GoalVault.sol:373 — why?] Why can the recipient take the pile the moment the total hits the target, before the deadline? Implicit belief: early success should close the drive. Combined with the clerk window, that belief also means an in-flight mint can land on a drive that can no longer label it.

[Socratic: GoalVault.sol:424 — why?] Why may the clerk force a cross-chain send? First answer: email-wallet UX. Why is that dangerous if the clerk also chose the source-chain tag? Implicit belief: the tag matches a chain where the same 20-byte address is the backer. On Solana (domain 5) that 20-byte padding is not their wallet.

[Socratic: GoalVault.sol:190 — why?] Why does `DeadlineTooFar` exist if `createCampaign` never uses it? Implicit belief at review time: deadlines would be capped so the rescue lock cannot be pushed to the end of `uint64`.

[Socratic: GoalVault.sol:452 — why?] Why is the destination caller left as the zero bytes? Implicit belief: anyone finalizing a mint to the backer is fine. Holds on EVM. Does not fix a wrong domain.

[Inversion: recordContribution] (1) After a miss, Alice refunds 600, clerk labels 100 of unlabeled USDC, Bob’s 300 cannot refund or be withdrawn — freeze. (2) After a miss and no refunds, clerk labels 100, recipient withdraws 1000 that includes coins never intended for this drive. (3) Clerk labels Alice’s mint to the clerk’s own campaign with goal=1, then withdraws.

[Inversion: withdraw] (1) Recipient withdraws the second the target is hit; a 15-minute mint then cannot be labeled and later becomes owner sweep. (2) Creator force-withdraws to a recipient that rejects tokens; labeled pile sticks (no refund fallback). (3) Withdraw after a grace label that used another drive’s mint.

[Inversion: refund] (1) Alice refunds on a miss; clerk then labels enough to “succeed”; remaining backers stick. (2) Alice’s local refund is sitting in the mempool; clerk force-`refundCrossChain`s her on a junk domain first. (3) Alice refunds; `raised` stays high so a later honest late-mint of 1 wei that crosses the target bricks the rest.

[Inversion: refundCrossChain] (1) Clerk tags sourceDomain=5, then forces the burn; Bob gets nothing on Arbitrum. (2) Owner swaps the shipping desk to a thief contract; clerk or Bob then calls this and the approval is stolen. (3) Owner sets fast-finality with a tiny maxFee so the burn always reverts; local `refund` still works, cross-chain path is dead.

[Inversion: rescueExcess] (1) Clerk never labels Alice’s mint; after the lock, owner sweeps it. (2) Early withdraw already made the mint unlistable; sweep takes it at lock time. (3) A stranger opens a drive with `deadline = type(uint64).max`; sweep is locked for the lifetime of the parameter.

[Inversion: createCampaign] (1) Open with `type(uint64).max` deadline to freeze rescue. (2) Open a 1-wei-goal drive as a vehicle for the clerk to label stray coins and withdraw. (3) Open with a recipient that cannot receive tokens so a later success has no exit.

[Inversion: contribute] (1) Give after the clerk already labeled enough to succeed and the recipient withdrew — closed, expected. (2) Give after the deadline while the clerk can still label — same-chain giver is locked out, clerk is not. (3) Direct-transfer USDC instead of this function; only the clerk or a later owner sweep can touch it.

[Inversion: _requireFailed] Looks sufficient (clock + under target + not withdrawn). Defeat: (1) clerk raises `raised` over target during the extra hour. (2) Same after a partial refund so `raised` is a lie about remaining coins. (3) Recipient already withdrew — correctly blocked, but that is the stranding path for late mints.

### RotatingVault

[Feynman: RotatingVault] A shared rotating savings table. An organizer picks the coin, the per-turn chip, how long a turn lasts, and how many seats. Seats are filled by the organizer’s signed tickets (anyone may hand the ticket in; rights stick to the name on the ticket). When every seat is filled the organizer rings the bell. Each turn, every name must put in exactly one chip. The pot for turn *i* pays only the name in seat *i*, and only if every name paid that turn. If a turn ends short, the table dies: unpaid pots melt back into “everyone takes home what they still have here,” minus chips that already left in a paid pot. There is no house owner. The organizer cannot kill the table after the bell — by design, so a winner cannot unwind the rest.

[Feynman: createCircle] Anyone opens a table; they become its organizer. Rejects zero coin, zero chip, zero/overlong turn, seat count outside 2..256, or a pot that would overflow.

[Feynman: redeemInvite] Anyone submits a ticket. Checks the table is filling, the name and seat are free, the ticket is unexpired and unused, and the signature is the organizer’s. Then the name owns that seat forever. The name never signs.

[Feynman: start] Organizer only, and only once every seat is taken. Sets the clock start to now. Turns are then just “how many turn-lengths have passed.”

[Feynman: cancel] Organizer only, only while filling, no coins ever held. Kills the table.

[Feynman: deposit / depositFor / _deposit] Pulls one chip from the *caller*, writes it as the *named member’s* payment for the *current* turn. Refuses a broken table, a non-member, a finished calendar, a second chip in the same turn, or a coin that does not deliver exactly one chip. A stranger paying for you can only add to your ledger, not take from it.

[Feynman: claim / claimFor / _claimTo] Pays seat *i*’s pot to that name only, and only if that turn is fully paid and the table is not dead. Anyone may push the payment to the name. Paying it increases “how many pots already left,” which lowers everyone’s later refund floor.

[Feynman: markBroken / _ensureBroken] Anyone may stamp a derived-dead table as stored-dead. Refunds do this too.

[Feynman: refund / refundFor / _refund] On a dead table, pays a name `their lifetime chips − (pots already paid × chip)`. Anyone may push that payment to the name.

[Feynman: _elapsedRounds] Whole turns since the bell, using now minus start over turn length.

[Feynman: _lazyBroken] Dead iff the most recently *finished* turn is short. Turn 0 still open ⇒ not dead. The write-up’s induction: you can only pay the live turn, and a dead table rejects new chips, so a later finished turn being short covers any earlier miss.

[Feynman: _firstFailedRound] Walks from seat 0 to find the first short turn, for the death event.

[Feynman: EIP-712 helpers / inviteDigest / domainSeparator / eip712Domain] Builds the ticket hash so a leaked ticket cannot be rewritten onto another name, chain, or contract. Recovers with ECDSA only.

[Feynman: views] Read stored/derived status, seats, current turn, windows, pot size, payee, funded flag, refundable amount.

[Socratic: RotatingVault.sol:447 — why?] Why is the refund floor `claimedCount * chip` for *every* name? First answer: every paid pot was funded by all N names. Implicit belief: a paid pot always consumed exactly one chip of *this* name. That holds because a pot cannot pay unless all N paid that turn — including this name, or someone who paid *for* them.

[Socratic: RotatingVault.sol:375 — why?] Why may a stranger push a pot to the payee? Implicit belief: getting paid cannot hurt the payee, and pushing it *before* a later miss is how you stop the pot from melting. The other side: a later name who wants the pot to melt just withholds their chip and hopes nobody pushes.

[Socratic: RotatingVault.sol:76 — why?] Why no organizer abort after the bell? Implicit belief: an organizer who already took an early pot would kill the table to freeze later winners. The inverse: the only unwind is an actual short turn, which a later name can cause on purpose to melt an unclaimed earlier pot.

[Socratic: RotatingVault.sol:226 — why?] Why does the ticket name never have to sign? Implicit belief: a leaked ticket can only enroll that name, and enrollment without their coin-approval cannot drain them. True for theft. They can still be seated without consent.

[Inversion: _claimTo] (1) Stranger `claimFor`s Alice in the last second of a healthy table so a coming miss cannot melt her pot. (2) Later names withhold; Alice is offline; nobody pushes; her pot melts. (3) Payee is a contract that rejects coins; `claimFor` always reverts; a later miss is the only unwind.

[Inversion: _deposit / depositFor] (1) A rich stranger pays the missing chip and stops a miss the others wanted (costs them one chip). (2) Sponsor pays everyone’s first turn, organizer in seat 0 takes the pot, sponsor is out the chips if the table then dies unclaimed. (3) Cannot pull Alice’s approval — pull is from the caller.

[Inversion: redeemInvite] (1) Front-run the same ticket — same name, no steal. (2) Change the name in the call — signature fails. (3) Enroll Alice without her asking; she owes nothing until she approves and pays.

[Inversion: _lazyBroken] (1) Skip a whole turn via a time jump — later turns have zero chips, last finished is short, table dies. (2) Pay only the live turn, so you cannot sneak-fund a future turn to hide an earlier miss. (3) After the last turn is fully paid, unfinished earlier pots stay claimable; death does not trigger.

[Inversion: start] (1) Organizer never rings — no coins locked. (2) One-second turns — members who still joined accepted that number. (3) Surprise bell does not pay the organizer unless every name still chips in.

[Inversion: _refund] (1) `refundFor` by a stranger only pays the name. (2) After a paid pot, a name who never chipped later turns gets zero (floor eats their only chip). (3) Death before any pot is paid returns every chip to its ledger name, including chips a sponsor put on that name.

## Dropped (single-lens or documented inherent)

- Relayer misattributes a mint to the wrong backer, in isolation — access-control / documented CCTP trust model, no second lens required to state.
- Early-pot winner then defaults in a later RotatingVault turn — header “honest trust notes,” inherent ROSCA, not a seam bug.
- Organizer assigns payoutIndex / never calls start / cancels while Filling — organizer doing organizer things; Filling holds no coins.
- Missing ERC-1271 for organizers — access-control / documented v2.
- Fee-on-transfer / rebasing unsupported — documented; deposit exact-delta is economic-security’s beat.
- Owner exists and can setRelayer — admin surface without an in-flight rewrite story beyond the setters already filed.
