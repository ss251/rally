## Tool protocol (scan)

[Feynman: RotatingVault] One contract holds many savings circles. An organizer opens a circle, members join with a signed invite that locks their payout turn, the organizer starts the clock, each live window every member puts in the same amount, and only when everyone has paid does that turn’s pot go to the person whose turn it is. If a window ends short, the circle dies and everyone pulls back whatever they still have sitting in the contract — minus the share that already left in pots that were fully paid out.

[Feynman: GoalVault] One contract holds many crowdfunds. Backers on the same chain send USDC in themselves. Backers on other chains burn USDC through Circle; a trusted relayer later writes down who that arriving USDC belongs to. If the pot hits the target, the beneficiary takes the whole credited pile. If time runs out short, each backer gets their credit back here or burned back to the chain they came from.

[Feynman: createCircle] Organizer names the token, the exact per-person amount, how long each turn lasts, and how many seats. The contract hands out the next id and stores those rules. No money moves.

[Feynman: redeemInvite] Anyone can submit a signed invite. The contract checks the circle is still filling, the seat is free, the person is new, the invite has a real expiry that has not passed, and the signature is the organizer’s. Then it fills that seat and burns the invite number.

[Socratic: RotatingVault.sol:251 — why?] Why reject only `expiresAt == 0` and not a far-future expiry? Implicit belief: the organizer’s signature is consent to that lifetime. A leaked invite with `expiresAt = max` lives until cancel.

[Feynman: start] Organizer, and only while filling and full, flips the circle to running and stamps the start time as “now.”

[Feynman: cancel] Organizer, only while filling (no money held), marks the circle dead. After start this path does not exist.

[Inversion: cancel] (1) Organizer starts, takes an early pot, then tries to cancel to unwind later members — blocked, no abort after start. (2) Organizer cancels a full circle before start — members lose seats, no funds at risk. (3) Stranger calls cancel — `NotOrganizer`.

[Feynman: _deposit] Only a running, not-dead circle. Credit goes to `member`. Tokens are pulled from the caller. The live turn is “how many full windows have passed.” One exact payment per person per turn. The pot counter ticks up. A short-received transfer is rejected.

[Feynman: deposit / depositFor] Same inner path. `deposit` pays for yourself. `depositFor` lets someone else pay and still writes the credit on `member`.

[Socratic: RotatingVault.sol:317 — why?] Why pull from the caller but credit `member`? Implicit belief: the payer is gifting the seat. Refunds later pay `member`, not the payer — the gift cannot be clawed back by the sponsor.

[Inversion: _deposit] (1) Pay a future turn in advance — `_elapsedRounds` pins the live turn only. (2) Pay after a failed earlier turn — `_lazyBroken` trips and rejects. (3) `depositFor` a non-member — `NotMember`.

[Feynman: _claimTo] Payee is a member; their seat number *is* the turn. That turn must be fully funded and not yet paid. Mark paid, bump the paid-turn counter, send `amount × N` to the payee. Last pot flips the circle to finished.

[Feynman: claim / claimFor] Same inner path. Anyone may push a payee’s pot to that payee.

[Inversion: _claimTo] (1) Claim someone else’s pot by passing their address — the turn is derived from *their* seat, so you pay *them*, never yourself. (2) Claim a funded pot after a later turn failed — `_lazyBroken` blocks; the pot dissolves into refunds. (3) Claim twice — `potClaimed` blocks.

[Feynman: _refund] Circle must be dead (stored or derived). Pay the member `totalDeposited − claimedCount × amount`. Write their deposited total down to that floor. Anyone may trigger it for them; tokens only go to them.

[Feynman: _lazyBroken] A circle is dead iff the most recently *finished* turn is short. Deposits only hit the live turn and stop the moment this trips, so a later finished turn being short means an earlier failure already happened (or this one is the first). The first window never counts as finished.

[Socratic: RotatingVault.sol:490 — why?] Why only the last finished turn, not a full scan? Implicit belief: nobody can fund a later turn after an earlier one failed. That holds only if every deposit path calls this check — both `deposit` and `depositFor` do.

[Inversion: _lazyBroken] (1) Fund turn 0, skip ahead several windows — later turns sit at 0, last-finished is short, circle dies, unclaimed turn-0 pot becomes refunds. (2) Try to fund turn 2 after turn 1 died — deposit sees dead and reverts. (3) All turns full, clock past the end — last-finished is full, not dead; deposits expire, claims still work.

[Feynman: GoalVault.createCampaign] Anyone opens a campaign with a target, a future deadline, and a beneficiary. Rescue-lock is pushed out to that deadline plus one hour.

[Socratic: GoalVault.sol:190 — why?] Why does `DeadlineTooFar` exist if `createCampaign` never uses it? Implicit belief at write time: a far deadline would pin `rescueUnlockTime` forever. The check was forgotten. The implicit belief now is “nobody will open a campaign at `uint64.max`.”

[Feynman: contribute] Same-chain backer, campaign still open (not withdrawn, now < deadline). Pull tokens, credit whatever actually arrived, tag it as the local domain.

[Feynman: recordContribution] Relayer only. Campaign not withdrawn and still inside deadline+1h. Vault balance must already cover existing escrow plus this amount. Credit the named backer and domain. No token pull.

[Socratic: GoalVault.sol:516 — why?] Why is the relayer window an hour looser than `contribute`? Implicit belief: a burn that left before the deadline may mint late, and that money should still count. Implicit belief that is *not* encoded: nobody has already been refunded, and `raised` still equals money still held for this campaign.

[Feynman: withdraw] Creator or beneficiary. Not yet swept. `raised >= goal`. Pay the *entire historical* `raised` to the beneficiary and drop global escrow by that same number.

[Feynman: _refund / refund / refundFor] Campaign missed (deadline passed, `raised < goal`, not swept). Zero the backer’s credit and every per-domain bucket. Drop global escrow by that credit. Send the full credit to the backer on this chain. **Do not touch `raised`.**

[Feynman: refundCrossChain] Backer or relayer. Same miss check. Zero the backer’s credit up front. Pay each domain locally or burn it back to the backer on that domain. **Do not touch `raised`.**

[Inversion: withdraw] (1) Sweep a miss — `raised < goal` reverts. (2) Sweep twice — `withdrawn` blocks. (3) After a miss-refund, a late credit pushes `raised` over the goal — `withdraw` pays the stale historical `raised`, which is larger than this campaign’s remaining escrow.

[Inversion: _requireFailed] (1) Refund before the deadline — blocked. (2) Refund after the goal is hit — blocked. (3) Refund after a miss, then a grace credit hits the goal — further refunds now see `raised >= goal` and die, even though money is still sitting here.

[Feynman: _requireOpen / _requireCreditable / _requireFailed] Three different doors. Same-chain in: before deadline, not swept. Relayer in: before deadline+1h, not swept. Refunds out: after deadline, `raised < goal`, not swept. From deadline to deadline+1h the last two doors are both open.

[Feynman: rescueExcess] Owner, after every campaign’s deadline+1h. Sweeps `balance − totalEscrowed` only.

[Inversion: rescueExcess] (1) Sweep during grace — locked. (2) Sweep escrowed money — only the slack is movable. (3) Open a campaign with deadline `uint64.max` — lock never lifts.

## Step 2–4 — Storage diffs that matter

**GoalVault `raised` vs `totalEscrowed` (broken mirror)**

- `_credit`: `raised += amount`, `totalEscrowed += amount`
- `_refund` / `refundCrossChain`: `totalEscrowed -= total`, `_contributedTotal = 0` — **`raised` untouched**
- `withdraw`: pays `raised` and does `totalEscrowed -= raised`

Same variable is the success oracle *and* the payout size, but only one of the two money-out paths updates it. RotatingVault does the mirror correctly: `totalDeposited` is reduced on refund; the claim payout is `A * N`, not a stale lifetime sum.

**GoalVault branch diff (`_requireOpen` vs `_requireCreditable` vs `_requireFailed`)**

| Check | contribute | recordContribution | refund* |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `withdrawn` | closed | closed | closed |
| `now >= deadline` | closed | **open until +1h** | **open** |
| `raised >= goal` | still open (overfund) | still open | closed |

Window `[deadline, deadline + 1 hours)`: refunds and late credits both succeed. That is the overlap that desynchronizes `raised` from remaining escrow.

**RotatingVault deposit/claim/refund write sets**

| Slot | deposit | claim | refund |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `roundDeposits[r][m]` | `= A` | — | — (stale after refund) |
| `roundFundedCount[r]` | `++` | — | — |
| `totalDeposited[m]` | `+= A` | — | `= claimedCount * A` |
| `potClaimed[r]` | — | `= true` | — |
| `claimedCount` | — | `+= 1` | read only |
| `status` | — | maybe Completed | Broken via `_ensureBroken` |

Claim and refund both consult `claimedCount` the same way; every claimed pot required `fundedCount == N`, so the floor identity holds. No RotatingVault write-set FINDING.

**Writer/reader lifecycle**

| Variable | Writers | Readers | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GoalVault `raised` | `_credit` only | withdraw, status, isSuccessful, isRefundable, `_requireFailed` | multiple readers treat it as “funds still here”; refund writers do not update it |
| GoalVault `DeadlineTooFar` | never | never | declared, unused |
| RotatingVault `totalDeposited` | deposit +=, refund = floor | refund, refundableAmount | mirrored |
| RotatingVault stored `status` | create/start/cancel/claim/_ensureBroken | mutators; `getCircle` raw | `circleStatus` / `refundableAmount` apply lazy break; `getCircle` does not |

## Cross-contract weaponize

The GoalVault `raised` bug is the inverse of RotatingVault’s refund floor. RotatingVault *does* decrement the lifetime credit (`totalDeposited = consumedFloor`) and *does not* pay a stale sum on the success path. No second copy of stale-raised-payout on RotatingVault.

GoalVault `contribute` credits `received` (fee-on-transfer shortfall allowed); RotatingVault reverts `UnexpectedTransferAmount`. Safer on the rotating side; GoalVault stays solvent because it credits the delta. Not a FINDING for USDC.

`claimFor` is permissionless (payee-only); GoalVault `refundFor` is `onlyRelayer`; `refundCrossChain` is backer|relayer. Different products, consistent “funds only to the named party.”

Invite `expiresAt` is still valid at the exact second (`timestamp > expiresAt`); campaign deadline is closed at the exact second (`timestamp >= deadline`). Inclusive/exclusive mismatch, no fund path.

## Admin variants

GoalVault `setRelayer` / `setTokenMessenger` / `setCctpRefundParams` / `rescueExcess` are owner-only. A malicious messenger or `cctpMaxFee >= amount` plus a relayer-forced `refundCrossChain` can burn value — trusted-owner surface, not reported (shared rule: admin-only doing admin things).

RotatingVault has no admin. Organizer `start`/`cancel` are not silent variants of deposit/claim; cancel is deliberately absent after start (header trust note). No Beefy-style unguarded admin rebalance.

---

## Bad symmetry

`DeadlineTooFar` is the unused twin of `DeadlineInPast` (FINDING 3).

RotatingVault `_deposit` / `_claimTo` check `status == Active` and then `_lazyBroken`. Once stored `Broken`, the first check fires. Redundant, not over-restrictive — refunds still open via `_ensureBroken`.

No `prepare`/`redeem` double-counter DoS.
